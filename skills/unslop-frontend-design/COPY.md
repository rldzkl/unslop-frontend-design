# Interface copy

Words appear in a design to make it easier to understand and use. Before writing, decide what the design needs to say and how saying it helps the person find their way. Briefs often arrive without real content, and the copy you invent can make a design feel as templated as its visuals.

- **The user's vocabulary.** Name things by what users understand, not by how the system is built: a user manages notifications, not webhook config. Say plainly what something is or does instead of selling it. Being specific and legible to new users beats being clever.
- **Actions say what happens.** Write in active voice. A call to action names its result: "Save changes", not "Submit". An action keeps its name through the whole flow, so "Publish" produces a "Published" toast. Consistent vocabulary is the signposting people learn the product by.
- **Errors and empty states give direction.** Say exactly what went wrong and how to fix it, in the interface's voice rather than a person's apology. An empty screen invites the next action.
- **Tone.** Conversational: plain verbs, sentence case, every word working, matched to the brand and audience. Each element does one job.
